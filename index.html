Educational resources for teaching open research to novices

Welcome!

Are you interested in conveying foundational open research knowledge and skills in the context of an open research course? Are you looking for teaching materials on a specific open research topic to use in your own teaching? Or are you new to open research as a whole and are trying acquire and practice some open research skills by yourself? Then you are in the right place!

On this page you will find ready-to-use materials and educational resources for teaching open research practices to novices or students on topics including foundations of Open Science, computational reproducibility, tools for collaborative working, data management and data sharing in a synchronous class-room setting (in-person, virtual or hybrid) or to use offline to learn at your own pace.

Who are these materials for?

Educators in higher higher education (e.g., universities, colleges, research institutions), looking for materials to teach a full course on open research or delivering individual workshops on specific open research topics to audiences with no or limited prior experience with open research, aiming to:

  • Integrate open research into an existing course syllabus or develop and deliver a full open research course
  • Develop a basic understanding of core principles in open research in their learners
  • Equip learners with foundational skills and experience with open research practices

Self-learners with no prior experience in open research, aiming to:

  • Develop basic open research skills
  • Obtain hands-on practical experience with core open research practices
  • Build-up on existing open research skills

What materials are there?

The materials consist of slide decks with theoretical elements, hands-on exercises, cheatsheets, homework assignments and practical tips on the content.

Each slide deck on a given topic is designed for a 90-minute session in a classroom setting (in-person, virtual, or hybrid), and can be flexibly adapted depending on your audience and timeframe.

Detailed presenter notes (intended narrative of the presentation), further instructor notes (additional guidance intended only for the presenter) and accessibility tips (where applicable) are available for each topic and are directly integrated into the materials.

The materials are completely free to use, given that you give appropriate credit.

TipAdapting the materials

You want to adapt the materials for your audience? The source code for the materials as well as PowerPoint files are available for download to tailor the materials to your exact audience.

How can I use these materials?

As an educator aiming to teach about open research: The materials are ready for immediate use in a classroom setting (timeframe: approx. 90 minutes per topic). You can choose to deliver a full course with the existing materials, or you can pick-and-choose specific open research topics to incorporate into an existing course. To access to materials, use the navigation menu on the left. The materials are arranged in a chronological order, meaning that they logically build up on one another. The slides can be directly presented in the browser in full screen or can be downloaded as pdf or MS PowerPoint if you want to edit them or present the materials offline. Presenter notes, instructor notes and accessibility tips provide important information about the content of the materials and valuable pedagogical tips how to convey the materials to your audience. If you opt for the pick-and-choose option, make sure to thoroughly read the prerequisites for each topic and go through the content and exercises yourself. If you re-use the materials for your own teaching, make sure to give appropriate credit to the original authors.

As a self-learner: Select your topic of choice from the navigation menu on the left. Follow along the slides and complete the practical exercises in your own time. You can navigate the slides in full screen using the arrow key, or by viewing the slides in the Speaker View together with the presenter and instructor notes to understand and reflect on the content of the materials (recommended, see here for more information). Make sure to always familiarize yourself with the prerequisites before starting a new topic. The materials can be downloaded as pdf or MS PowerPoint documents. Note: Group exercises are not possible in an asynchronous setting, but can be completed individually.

ImportantRead the introductory page for each topic

When selecting a topic, you will be redirected to a separate website with all the materials. Please ensure to read the About these materials section, as well as details about the intended target audience, the prerequisites to complete the topic and the learning objectives. You can also find the contributor list, the license details and the citation statement on each website.

How can I adapt the materials?

Of course, the context in which you are teaching may vary greatly and you may need to adapt the materials in terms of your audience, content, level of difficulty or timeframe. If you want to adapt the materials, you can export the slides as MS PowerPoint (.pptx format) corresponding to the materials and adapt the materials to your specific teaching context. Make sure to give appropriate credit to the original authors. You also have the option to export the slides in pdf format.

In addition, you can also obtain the materials from our LMU OSC GitHub repository. You can clone and fork the repository to adapt the materials to your needs, or create a new branch to work on the materials in parallel. Within this repository, you have direct access to the Quarto .qmd files that support all of our materials.

How can I give feedback on the materials?

Have you spotted a way to improve our materials, or would like to add or contribute to the materials? We would love to hear your feedback! There are different ways to give feedback:

  • The quick and easy way: Contact the program coordinator Sarah von Grebmer.

  • The pro way (involves GitHub): Navigate to the GitHub repository and open an issue, see here for instructions on how to do this. Provide a descriptive title for your feedback, and describe your feedback in greater detail in the text box. Include which materials you want to provide feedback on, what the issue is and if it is low, medium or high priority. Tag Sarah von Grebmer (using her GitHub username SvonGrebmer).

  • No prior open research knowledge is required! You can first acquire the open research skills yourself by going through the slides and practical activities, or you can also complete the self-paced tutorials on these topics in the self learning catalogue. In addition, you can develop your teaching skills here.
  1. Who can I teach with these materials?
  • The materials can be flexibly adapted to bachelor students, master students or early career researchers (PhD, PostDocs) who are new to open research or have not had prior training on these topics.
  1. I am not a member of the LMU Open Science Center, can I still teach with these slides?
