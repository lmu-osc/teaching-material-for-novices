Recap of key concepts in preregistration part I

Study preregistration is an open science practice that:

  • safe guards researchers from questionable research practices and biases by making decision-making processes more explicit.

  • involves uploading a time-stamped, publicly-available* record of a research study plan onto a register, which is given a unique and permanent study identifier.

  • *Preregistrations do not need to be made public immediately and can be embargoed for a period of years (e.g. Open Science Framework preregistrations can be embargoed for 4 years from first submission)

Presenter Notes: Study preregistration is an open science practice that safe guard researchers from questionable research practices and biases by making decision-making processes more explicit.

Section 1: Benefits and barriers of preregistration

Presenter Notes: In section 1, we will go over the key benefits and barriers of preregistration. We will first consider how benefits and opportunities of preregistration exist from individual researchers all the way to the wider research ecosystem.

Benefits for researchers I

Improved planning and project management

  • The process of filling out preregistration document helps identify key information needed at study design stage
  • Outlining data collection methods and analysis plans which reduces ambiguity and errors during data analysis stage

Builds your reputation

  • Transparently show to the wider research community your plans and tests
  • Signal that you have taken steps to mitigate against hindsight and confirmation bias (Wagenmakers & Dutilh, 2016).

Presenter Notes: A key area that preregistration benefits researchers is by improving the planning and project management as time is spent outlining data collection methods and analysis plans which reduces ambiguity and errors during data analysis stage. The process of filling out preregistration document helps identify key information needed at study design stage, which is particularly helpful for early career researchers.

Preregistrations also allow you to build your reputation by - Transparently showing to the wider research community your plans and tests and - signalling that you have taken steps to mitigate against hindsight and confirmation bias.

Benefits for researchers II

Easier to write up and publish findings

  • Front loading the thinking and planning makes writing up results and discussion a smoother process

  • If you preregister your analytic code on simulated data the results of your confirmatory analyses will be generated quicker once you have collected and cleaned data

  • If preregistration is approved as a registered report during stage 1 journal peer review, you have “in-principle acceptance”

Visible productivity

  • Publicly sharing outputs that others can evaluate beyond a journal article (as evaluation of outputs can be influenced by journal prestige)

Presenter Notes: Preregistration makes it easier to write up and publish findings by front loading the thinking and planning. This makes writing up results and discussion a smoother process!

If you preregister your analytic code on simulated data the results of your confirmatory analyses will be generated quicker once you have collected and cleaned data

And, if preregistration is approved as a registered report during stage 1 journal peer review, you have “in-principle acceptance”

Closely linked, is the benefit of more visibility of your work beyond journal articles (as evaluation of outputs can be influenced by journal prestige).

Benefits for teams

Clearer involvement of collaborators/project partners

  • Provides opportunities for project partners to discuss and refine plans before data collection
  • Detailed research plans provide clarity for project partners, which is increasingly important in the era of large-scale collaborations

Presenter Notes: Clearer involvement of collaborators/project partners is another important benefit of preregistration at the level of teams.

Preregistration provides opportunities for project partners to discuss and refine plans before data collection - a chance to catch any potential errors early!

Detailed research plans provide clarity for project partners, which is increasingly important in the era of large-scale collaborations (also known as Big Team Science).

Benefits to wider research ecosystem

Increase research credibility and bias

  • Mitigates against questionable research practices
  • Offers more complete reporting and transparency of research project
  • Reduces publication bias, as preregistration/registered report is available to read (if not embargoed)

Check thinking

  • Other people reading your findings can follow your thought process
  • Gain a deeper understanding of a project and make an informed assessment (Binney et al., 2025)

Presenter Notes: Thinking at the scale of academic disciplines and wider academia, preregistration helps in two main ways:

firstly by increasing research credibility by - Mitigating against questionable research practices and - Offering more complete reporting and transparency of research project. - by mitigating against the publication bias or file drawer effect as the project plans are available and accessible on a repository or published as a registered report even if there are no statistically significant results. This is especially valuable when it comes to meta-analyses.

Secondly, it allows other people to check thinking by - Allowing other people to read your findings and follow your thought process enabling them to - gain a deeper understanding of a project.

Instructor Notes: Note about research credibility in slide at the end of section 1 about PARKing (“Preregistrating after the results are known”).

Benefits recap

  • There are many benefits to doing a preregistration for individual researchers, teams and the wider research ecosystem.

  • Overall, by taking the time to carefully plan your study design, data collection and approach to analysis, research becomes more rigorous, transparent and credible.

Barriers to preregistration

Wider context:

  • There are significant challenges present in the current research and assessment culture (e.g. “publish or perish”) which influences the uptake of preregistration.

  • Researchers have different knowledge and experience levels with Open Science practices that reflect in attitudes and adoption of preregistration.

Presenter Notes: In the final part of section 1 we will outline some common challenges researchers new to the practice of preregistration can encounter.

Some context to keep in mind:

  • There are significant challenges present in the current research and assessment culture (e.g. “publish or perish”) which influences the uptake of preregistration.

  • Researchers have different knowledge and experience levels with Open Science practices that reflect in attitudes and adoption of preregistration.

Challenges: time

Time: “I haven’t got time to do a preregistration”

  • Reframing: front-loaded effort during the planning stage can help later on during write up and during peer review (remember the benefits to researchers slide earlier)

Challenges: statistical confidence

Statistical confidence: “How do I know my planned statistical analysis is the right one?”

  • Strategy: Simulate random data with the distribution and sample size of expected real data and run the statistical test planned for the study.
  • Repeat the statistical test (e.g. 100 x) and check results are within the false-positive finding rate of 5%
  • Run a power analysis by simulating data with the expected effect size for the study and check model registers effect at least 80% of the time

Challenges: inflexible

Lack of flexibility: “Preregistration keeps you in a box”

  • Reframing: Preregistrations are a “plan not a prison” as you can document any deviations from your plan when you write up the research findings (DeHaven, 2017).
  • Preregistrations can be tailored to the study type (e.g incremental
  • transparently documenting exploratory-confirmatory pathway or sequential - multi-stage contingent experiments).

Find out more

Binney et al. (2025) discuss practical guidance of balancing mitigating biases with flexibility in the research process. Lakens (2024) discusses when and how to deviate from a preregistration.

Presenter Notes: “Preregistration keeps you in a box” is another common perceived barrier. There is different degrees of flexibility in preregistations as you can document any deviations from your plan when you write up the research findings. It is important to describe changes to the design, sampling or analysis plans clearly.

Preregistration can be tailored to the study type. They can be incremental (e.g. transparently documenting exploratory-confirmatory pathway) or sequential (e.g. multi-stage contingent experiments).

Challenges: scooping?

Fear of scooping: “What if someone steals my ideas before they are published?”

  • Counterargument: Preregistrations do not need to be made public right away and can be embargoed for a period of years (e.g. Open Science Framework preregistrations can be embargoed for 4 years from first submission)

  • The embargo period allows researchers time after preregistering their study to conduct the research, analyse, write up and publish their findings.

Presenter Notes: A commonly cited barrier to preregistration is tied to intellectual property with the fear of being scooped. The pressure to publish contributes to the fear of having research ideas “stolen” before they are published, as preregistrations are publically available.

To counteract this barrier, preregistered studies can be uploaded onto preregistration platforms and made private for a period of time. The Open Science Framework allows an embargo period of 4 years from first submission before the preregistration is made public.

The embargo period allows researchers time after preregistering their study to conduct the research, analyse, write up and publish their findings.

Challenges: lack of support

Lack of support: “No one in my team knows or cares about preregistration”

  • Strategy: Often a reluctance to try out preregistration comes from a place of lack of familiarity with the process.

  • Sharing resources, and suggest a group meeting to discuss/learn more about preregistration to increase familiarity.

  • Reframe preregistration as a creative opportunity to plan great science! Planning is where the science is, as other research processes, like data collection can be delegated

What preregistration is not

Preregistration does not prevent fraud or research misconduct (e.g. PARKing), but it does make it harder.


Definition

PARKing: “Preregistrating after the results are known” is considered research misconduct where researchers complete an experiment before preregistering which invalidates the purpose of preregistration (Parsons et al., 2022).
